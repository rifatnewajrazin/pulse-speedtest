Neither is wrong. They measure different paths with different methods, so the numbers differ. Pulse is shown in the last column so you can see where it fits.

Fast.com is lower when…

Your ISP is congested toward Netflix, the Netflix box near you is busy, or Wi-Fi is weak. It reflects what streaming really gets.

Ookla is higher when…

A big local server exists (ISPs often host them, so traffic stays on their fast network). It shows line capacity, not everyday experience.

Which should you trust?

Use Ookla to check you get the plan you pay for. Use Fast.com to know how streaming will feel. A big gap between them can hint at ISP throttling of video.

Glossary — the confusing bits

Megabit (Mb) vs Megabyte (MB)

A bit is the smallest unit of data. A byte is 8 bits. Internet providers advertise speed in megabits per second (Mbps) because the number looks bigger. Your browser and download managers show megabytes per second (MB/s). Small b = bit, capital B = byte.

Mega, Giga — how big?

1 Gigabit = 1,000 Megabits. 1 Gigabyte = 1,000 Megabytes = 8,000 Megabits. So a 1 Gbps line downloads at most 125 MB/s.

Download vs upload

Download = getting data to you (streaming, browsing, game updates). Upload = sending data out (video calls, cloud backups, posting videos). Most home plans have much slower upload.

Ping / latency

How long a tiny message takes to reach a server and come back, in milliseconds (ms). Lower is better. Under 30 ms is excellent, under 60 ms is fine for gaming, over 150 ms feels laggy.

Jitter

How much your ping bounces around. Low jitter = smooth calls and games. High jitter = choppy voice, rubber-banding in games.

Packet loss

Data travels in small packets. If some never arrive they must be re-sent, causing stutter. Anything above 1% is worth fixing; above 2.5% hurts calls and games a lot. Weak Wi-Fi is the usual cause.

Bufferbloat

When your connection is busy, ping can shoot up because packets queue behind each other. Ping during download much higher than idle ping means one person's download makes everyone else lag.

Real, shared and CGNAT IPs

Your public IP is your address on the internet. Some providers put many customers behind one address (CGNAT, range 100.64.x.x) — that's a shared IP: fine for browsing, but a problem for hosting servers, port forwarding and some games. A VPN or proxy replaces your real IP with the VPN server's one.

Why is my speed lower than my plan?

Wi-Fi (walls, distance, 2.4 GHz), many devices at once, an old router, or ISP congestion at busy hours. Try again over an Ethernet cable and compare — that shows your real line speed without Wi-Fi in the way.
